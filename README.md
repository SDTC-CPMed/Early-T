# A Prototypic Population-to-Single-Cell Framework for Early Cancer Trajectory Prediction and Prevention  

 

Abstract 

Early diagnosis and treatment of cancer in patients with risk-diseases may greatly improve prognosis, but is complicated by lack of specific symptoms, biomarkers and drug targets. Prognosis could be improved by precision biomarkers and drug targets to prevent such cancer trajectories (CTs). This would require solving multiple problems on epidemiological and molecular scales, which may vary in different countries. Because those scales are inter-dependent, they should ideally be studied together on a global scale. This would be far beyond the scope of one study. Here, we instead present a three-pronged epidemiological-molecular framework to construct CTs (TWIN-MAP). We propose that this may serve as a starting point for AI-based early diagnosis and treatment of CTs: Prong 1) Definition of globally shared CTs leading to the most common cancers based on two large cohort studies and curation of electronic health records (EHR) by a global team of oncologists; Prong 2) Construction of molecular models of the most common shared CTs; Prong 3) Identification of biomarkers and drug targets for those CTs. In Prongs 1 and 2, combined analyses of EHR from two different countries, and profiling data showed great complexity and heterogeneity of the CTs. Because of this, and that cancers may develop many years before clinical diagnosis, we hypothesized that biomarkers for early cancer diagnosis could be prioritized by scRNA-seq of epithelial cells from tumors. We focused on the three most common cancers; Colorectal, lung and prostate. In Prong 3, machine-learning supported that biomarkers inferred from the single cell analyses, in combination with known risk factors, had potential for early cancer diagnosis many years before clinical cancer diagnosis. From a therapeutic perspective we found that the genes inferred from the single cell analyses were  significantly enriched for drugs approved for treatment of each cancer, as well as several novel candidates with low toxicities and costs. We propose adaption and scaling TWIN-MAP for early prediction, prevention, or treatment of CTs on scales ranging from populations to individuals, and identify key challenges to reach these aims. We make TWIN-MAP, as well as all data and methods available for such studies.
